## 5. Questions they will likely ask about your resume

**Q: Your resume is policy-heavy. Why consulting at a power markets firm?**
A: Policy is the reason storage projects succeed or fail. I want to turn policy knowledge into market analysis and regulatory advice for clients: tariffs, tenders, open access, and storage economics.

**Q: Which parts of the FTM and BTM reports did you write?**
A: Name the policy, regulation and state-comparison sections you worked on, then add one insight: for example, FTM is tender-driven (FDRE tariffs of about ₹4.4 to 5.6/kWh, peak power about ₹3.4/kWh), while BTM is reliability-driven (33 GWh in 2023, inverters shrinking).

**Q: What is a key finding from the FTM report?**
A: Utility-scale storage dominates (93 to 96% of the market); cumulative potential is about 173 GWh by 2032 in the base case; the NEP says India needs about 236 GWh of batteries by 2031-32; policy gaps include no storage-specific policy and weak time-of-day tariffs.

**Q: What is a key finding from the BTM report?**
A: BTM was 33 GWh in 2023 and grows only about 3% a year to about 44 GWh by 2032; lead-acid is over 98% outside telecom; time-of-day tariffs and falling LFP prices ($156 to $94/kWh) are the main enablers.
