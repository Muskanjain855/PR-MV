# Grid & Battery Storage: One-Page Study Sheet + Quiz

## Part 1: Study Sheet

| Topic | Simple meaning | Remember this |
|---|---|---|
| Grid frequency | Heartbeat of the grid | 50 Hz, band ~49.90–50.05 Hz. Low = demand > supply. High = supply > demand |
| Discom | Bulk buyer, sells to customers | Orders power in advance (schedule) |
| DSM | Fine for deviating from schedule | Bigger fine when the grid is stressed. Batteries reduce deviation |
| Peak hour | Evening rush (~6–10 pm) | Solar is gone, demand is highest, power costs most |
| Energy shifting | Store at noon, use at night | Also called arbitrage. Flattens the duck curve |
| FDRE | Solar + wind + storage with a delivery promise | "Firm" + "Dispatchable". Run by SECI, NTPC, NHPC, SJVN |
| Co-located solar + storage | Battery at the same site as solar | Shares the grid connection. Cuts curtailment |
| Primary reserve | Instant automatic reaction | Seconds. Governor action |
| Secondary reserve | Restores frequency (AGC) | Minutes |
| Tertiary reserve | Operator-dispatched backup | 15+ minutes |
| Black start | Restart the grid with no outside power | Hydro, diesel, batteries. Past blackouts: 2001, 2012 |
| Ancillary Services Regs | CERC 2022 rules for grid-support services | Lets batteries get paid for services |
| VGF | Government pays part of project cost | First scheme 4,000 **MWh** (not MW). Second round ~30 GWh (verify) |
| ACC PLI | Incentive for making batteries in India | ~50 GWh, ₹18,100 crore |
| MP state rules | Madhya Pradesh (MPERC) | Solar-heavy state, pushing solar + storage tenders (verify latest orders) |
| RPO / RCO | Must buy/consume X% renewable | RPO became RCO under the Energy Conservation Act |
| ESO | Must have X% storage-backed energy | ~1% (2023–24) rising to ~4% (2029–30). Confirm latest |
| FTM | Grid side of the meter | Big. Serves the grid. Earns from auctions, tariffs, services |
| BTM | Customer side of the meter | Small. Serves one customer. Saves on bills |

### Quick memory tricks
- MW = how fast (power). MWh = how much (energy). Water-tap speed vs. bucket size.
- FTM = Fixes The Many (the grid). BTM = Bills To Minimize (the customer).
- Primary, secondary, tertiary = reflex, rebalance, reinforcements.

### Caveats to verify before using in an interview or report
- Official CERC timings for reserves differ from the 5 / 5–10 / 15–30 min notes: primary is seconds, secondary ~30 s to 15 min, tertiary 15+ min.
- VGF, ESO percentages and ISTS-waiver dates change often. Check the latest MoP/CERC notifications.

## Part 2: Q&A Quiz (answers below)

1. What is India's nominal grid frequency?
2. What does DSM stand for, and why can a discom be penalized under it?
3. What is energy shifting, and when is peak hour?
4. What does each word in FDRE mean?
5. Give two benefits of co-locating storage with solar.
6. Name the three types of ancillary service reserve in order of speed.
7. What is black start, and which resources can do it?
8. The VGF scheme is 4,000 MW. True or false?
9. What does ACC PLI support?
10. How does RPO differ from ESO?
11. Give one use of FTM storage and one use of BTM storage.
12. Why are batteries better than coal plants for frequency response?

### Answers
1. 50 Hz.
2. Deviation Settlement Mechanism. A discom is penalized for drawing more or less than its schedule, especially when the grid is stressed.
3. Storing cheap midday solar and using it at the evening peak (~6–10 pm).
4. Firm (reliable), Dispatchable (available on command), Renewable, Energy.
5. Shared grid connection and land (lower cost), and less wasted solar (curtailment). It also allows evening sales at better prices.
6. Primary, then secondary, then tertiary.
7. Restarting the grid without external power. Hydro, diesel generators and batteries can do it.
8. False. It is 4,000 MWh (energy), not MW (power).
9. Domestic manufacturing of advanced chemistry cell batteries.
10. RPO requires buying renewable energy. ESO requires a share of energy to be backed by storage.
11. FTM: energy shifting or ancillary services. BTM: peak shaving or lower bills.
12. Batteries respond in milliseconds. Coal plants take much longer to ramp.
